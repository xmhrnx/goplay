GoPlay

A video player for people who just want to watch something — with depth for those who want to tune everything.

It plays almost any file or stream with mpv's engine, in a quiet, near-black interface that gets out of the picture's way.

What it does

Open it. Press play.

Everything below is in the box: no plugins to hunt for, no codec packs. The quiet interface stays out of the way until you want it.

0100:00:00

Watching

  • Nearly every video and audio format, local or from a link — YouTube, Vimeo, Twitch and the other sites yt-dlp knows, plus HLS/DASH streams.
  • Hardware decoding everywhere (VideoToolbox, D3D11, VA-API), drawn straight from the GPU.
  • Resumes where you left off; one episode queues the rest of the folder in natural order.
  • DVDs and Blu-rays from a drive, a disc folder or an .iso.
0200:12:30

Controls

  • In a window, controls sit above and below the picture — never over it. Fullscreen, they float in from the edges.
  • Timeline with chapters, buffered ranges, an A–B loop and a preview under the pointer.
  • Speed from 0.25× to 4× with pitch kept, frame stepping, shuffle, go to a time.
  • Picture-in-picture and always on top.
0300:27:05

Picture & sound

  • Video, audio and subtitle tracks, a second subtitle line, external files.
  • Subtitle size, position, color, outline, delay and encoding.
  • Aspect, zoom, rotation, deinterlacing, debanding, smooth motion, HDR tone mapping.
  • Equalizer presets, loudness normalization, night mode, surround passthrough.
0400:41:52

3D & VR

  • Side-by-side and top-and-bottom 3D, shown as anaglyph, 2D, interleaved, checkerboard or passed to a 3D TV.
  • 360° and VR180 video — equirectangular, fisheye, YouTube's cubemap: drag to look around.
  • Recognizes 3D and 360° files by name.
0500:58:19

Casting

  • Chromecast, Google TV, Android TV and DLNA/UPnP renderers, found automatically.
  • Files the TV can't play are repackaged or converted on the fly with FFmpeg; subtitles go along.
  • Stop casting and pick up here where the TV left off.
0601:13:44

Your network

  • Browse what DLNA/UPnP media servers share — a NAS, Plex, Emby, Jellyfin, Windows media sharing.
  • Play a single file or a whole folder.
  • A server that isn't found by itself can be added by its address.

Nothing runs unless something changed. A paused player uses no CPU; during playback, frames are drawn at the video's own rate.

Download

Get GoPlay.

Each release is built and tested on macOS (Apple silicon), and on Windows and Linux for both x64 and ARM64.

macOS

Apple silicon. Drag GoPlay into Applications — libmpv is inside.

.dmg

Windows

x64 and ARM64. An installer for the current user, or a portable folder.

-setup.exe.zip

Linux

x64 and ARM64. Packages that use your system's libmpv, or a tarball.

.deb.rpm.tar.gz

Optional at runtime: yt-dlp for web pages, FFmpeg for casting files a TV can't play, and on Linux without a desktop portal, zenity for folder dialogs. To build it yourself, see the README.

Privacy policy

Your videos stay yours.

GoPlay has no accounts, ads, analytics, tracking or crash reporting, and it sends nothing to its developer. This policy covers GoPlay on macOS, Windows and Linux, wherever you got it: GitHub, the Mac App Store or the Microsoft Store.

What stays on your computer

Your settings, the recent files list (up to 30 entries, with how far you got) and resume positions, network media servers you add by address, and your own mpv.conf, input.conf and scripts. They're kept in GoPlay's folder: ~/Library/Application Support/goplay on macOS (inside the app's container in the Mac App Store version), %AppData%\goplay on Windows and ~/.config/goplay on Linux.

Screenshots you take are saved in your Pictures folder. Clear Recent empties the list; deleting the folder removes everything.

What goes over the network

Only what you ask for. A link or stream you open is fetched straight from its site, which sees your IP address as with any download; web pages are read by yt-dlp if you've installed it.

Casting and network browsing stay on your local network: GoPlay looks for Chromecast, DLNA and media server devices there, and serves the file you cast to the device you chose. GoPlay doesn't check for updates or contact any server of its own.

Who else gets data

No one, from GoPlay. Sites you stream from, and the store you installed GoPlay from, have their own privacy policies; Apple and Microsoft may share anonymous install and crash statistics with the developer if you allowed that in your system settings.

Children, changes and contact

GoPlay collects no personal information from anyone, children included. If this policy changes, the new version will be on this page with a new date.
